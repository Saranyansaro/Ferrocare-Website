## 2. Technology — how the two core processes work

### 2.1 Electrostatic Liquid Cleaning (ELC) — the flagship technology

Source: current ELC catalogue (`_files/ugd/46b5d6_c12bdc…pdf`) and the ELC page on ferrocare.net.

- Working principle: **"KLEENTEK FERROCARE ELC uses the principle of Electrostatics to separate and collect fluid contaminants."** A special transformer charges two sets of stainless-steel electrodes, one positive and one negative. As fluid flows freely through the cleaning cell, suspended impurities migrate to the electrodes and deposit on the enormously spread surface of the **dielectric media / collector**; maximum collection occurs at the pleated collector edges where the deformed electric field is strongest.
- Removes suspended impurities **down to sub-micron levels**, plus **sludge and varnish** that mechanical filters do not "see" as particles. The catalogue states "Theoretically, even particles below 0.01 µ can be removed" and that the ELC removes all sizes and types of particles **including bacteria and virus**. Electrostatic forces act on metal wear, paper, wood, plastic and rubber particles alike.
- **Operating conditions (stated):** mineral base oils **except IC-engine oil**; maximum moisture content **500 ppm**; maximum temperature **60 °C**; standard power source **415 V, 3-phase, 50 Hz**; special specifications available for synthetic oils and for high-temperature / fire-resistant fluids.
- Model selection criteria (per ferrocare.com `about-8`): fluid volume, fluid viscosity, application, and sophistication of the hydraulic system (servo valves, proportional valves, etc.).
- Current-generation **ELC R Series** is described as "Engineered with enhanced Capacity & Speed" and **IoT enabled**; all units are offered with **IOT & MODBUS processors** transmitting data to an Android phone or computer in real time.

### 2.2 Low Vacuum Dehydration & Degassification (LVDH)

Source: current LVDH catalogue (`_files/ugd/46b5d6_9f8195…pdf`), `ferrocare.com/general-clean`, and the archived 2016 catalogue.

- Working principle: **oil is indirectly heated and fed into a vacuum chamber where it is dispersed into a thin film to vaporise water and dissolved gases at low temperature.** Under vacuum, water boils below **53 °C**. A refrigerated condenser collects the evaporated water; dissolved air and gases are bled to atmosphere via the vacuum pump.
- Claimed benefits: no burn-off, no oxidation, no additive depletion because heating is indirect and at low temperature; reduces water content **in a single pass by 0.25 %–1.5 %** depending on inlet oil temperature; ultimate water removal through recirculation to **zero free water content, or 50 % below the oil's saturation level**; soluble gases and air reduced to 50 % of saturation; also removes **cracked low-molecular-weight oil** produced by oxidation, restoring oil toward original properties.
- Company FAQ states LVDH-600 is the method for on-line water removal "without taking the oil to high temperatures", and that it "can reduce moisture content to below the saturation limit".
- Application areas (stated): hydraulic oils, turbine oils, lubricating oils, gear oils, compressor oils, vacuum pump and compressor seal oils, and dehydration before blending different oils.

### 2.3 Integrated ELC + LVDH platform

"The Integrated Unit" combines both processes in one skid: removes free and emulsified moisture **and** suspended particulate from hydraulic and lubricating oils, with automatic reading of moisture (ppm or water solubility) and particulate purity (NAS/ISO), readout on an Android phone/computer in real time, and a self-diagnostic touch screen.
